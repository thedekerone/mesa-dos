Weight loss · two people · Finnish supermarkets

Peruvian, Asian and European home cooking — sized for losing weight, not for starving.

Fourteen dinners on a two-week rotation, protein-forward breakfasts, lunches that are mostly leftovers, and one shopping list per week that you can actually fill at S-market, K-Market or Lidl.

How the days are built

Breakfast ~380 kcal · lunch ~430 kcal · dinner ~480 kcal · snack ~160 kcal — about 1 450–1 650 kcal a day and 95–130 g protein, depending on the day. The lighter days carry a top-up line, so the week averages roughly 1 550 kcal a person. That is a 400–600 kcal daily deficit, or about 0.4–0.6 kg a week for most adults. Both of you eat the same dinner; only the plate size changes. Lunch is almost always last night's dinner, which is why the shopping list is short.

No calorie counting at the table: fill half the plate with vegetables, a quarter protein, a quarter starch. Weigh once a week, in the morning, same conditions.

    How to actually run this

    The plan is the easy part. This page is the part that decides whether it survives week three.

    1. The plate rule

    Half vegetables, a quarter protein (150–200 g cooked), a quarter starch (about one fist). Protein first, vegetables second, starch last — you eat less without noticing. This single rule replaces most calorie counting.

    2. Protein is the lever

    Aim for 25–35 g at every meal. In Finnish shops the cheap, boring workhorses are maitorahka (quark, ~11 g/100 g), kanan filee, kirjolohi, pakasteseiti, kananmuna, raejuusto and tonnikala vedessä. They do more for hunger than any "light" product on the shelf.

    3. Cook two portions of dinner, eat one for lunch

    Every dinner here is written for two and holds up as next-day lunch. That is the whole trick to a two-person plan: you cook once, you eat twice, you never stand in front of the fridge at 12:30 deciding.

    4. Where to buy what

    • S-market / Prisma (Pirkka, Kotimaista) — best everyday base: chicken, quark, rye bread, frozen fish, own-brand spices.
    • K-Market — smaller packs, better when you only need 300 g of something; Pirkka "Kevyt" dairy is solid.
    • Lidl — cheapest for vegetables, nuts, olive oil, feta, tinned beans and frozen berries. Their weekly "teemaviikko" often carries Asian staples.
    • Ethnic aisle (etninen hylly) in Prisma/Citymarket, or Vii Voan / Hakaniemi market in Helsinki — aji amarillo paste, gochujang, curry paste, proper soy sauce, rice noodles, cheap limes and coriander.
    • Pakasteet — frozen fish, berries, edamame and spinach are the budget cheat codes. Frozen fish is fine for ceviche-style dishes if you thaw it in the fridge.

    5. What we deliberately do not do

    • No "detox", no meal-replacement shakes, no skipping dinner.
    • No deep frying. The oven does the work — lomo saltado keeps its character with oven fries.
    • No exotic ingredient that only exists online. If aji amarillo paste is a hassle, the substitution is in the recipe.

    6. Two people, two appetites

    Use the plate setting at the top: Lighter = 0.8×, Standard = 1×, Hearty = 1.2×. If one of you is hungrier, that person takes the hearty plate and the other the lighter one — same food, same cooking, different arithmetic. Nobody needs a separate meal.

    7. The week in one line

    Shop Sunday, cook Sunday and Wednesday, eat leftovers the rest of the time. If a day goes sideways, repeat a dinner from earlier in the week — the plan is a rotation, not a contract.
