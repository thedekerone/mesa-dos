How this works

One pot, two plates

We eat the same food. The recipes are written in standard portions, and each of us gets that scaled to a daily target, so one plate is bigger than the other. Nobody cooks a separate meal. The bar on every recipe shows how to split the pan.

Cook once, eat twice

Where tomorrow's lunch is tonight's dinner, the dinner is cooked double and the day says so. Box the lunch portions before sitting down, or they get eaten.

The plate rule

Half vegetables, a quarter protein, a quarter starch. Protein first, vegetables second, starch last. This one rule replaces most calorie counting at the table.

Protein is the lever

Aim for 25–35 g at every meal. In Finnish shops the cheap, boring workhorses are maitorahka (quark, about 11 g per 100 g), kanan filee, kirjolohi, pakasteseiti, kananmuna, raejuusto and tonnikala vedessä. They do more for hunger than any “light” product on the shelf.

What we do not do

  • No detox, no meal-replacement shakes, no skipping dinner.
  • No deep frying. The oven does the work, and lomo saltado keeps its character with oven fries.
  • No ingredient that only exists online. If aji amarillo paste is a hassle, the substitution is in the recipe.

The week in one line

Shop at the weekend, cook most evenings, eat last night's dinner for lunch. Every week is dealt fresh from all the dinners, so nothing comes back for at least three weeks. If a day goes sideways, swap two days or repeat a dinner. The plan is a suggestion, not a contract.

The scale

Once a week, same morning, same conditions. Daily numbers are noise. When 4–5 kg have gone, work the target out again: a lighter body needs less.

Nutrition figures are estimates from standard ingredient values. Prices and product names change constantly. Not medical advice: with a condition that affects diet, run the plan past a doctor or a dietitian first.
